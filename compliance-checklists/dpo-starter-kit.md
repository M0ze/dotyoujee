# Data Protection Officer (DPO) Starter Kit

Essential steps and templates for organizations beginning their journey under Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act (2019).

> **Disclaimer:** This kit provides general guidance. Legal review is recommended before submission to the Personal Data Protection Office (PDPO).

---

## Step 1: Determine if you need to register

You likely need PDPO registration if your organization:

- Collects personal data from customers, employees, or users in Uganda
- Processes data for marketing, HR, payments, health, or education
- Operates a website or app that stores user information
- Acts as a data controller or processor for third parties

**Action:** Review PDPO registration requirements at [pdpo.go.ug](https://pdpo.go.ug) and prepare your application documents.

---

## Step 2: Appoint internal accountability

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|----------------|
| **Data Protection Officer (or lead contact)** | Oversees compliance, liaises with PDPO, handles data subject requests |
| **Executive sponsor** | Approves budget and policy decisions |
| **IT lead** | Implements technical controls (encryption, access, logging) |

- [ ] Named individual assigned as DPO or compliance lead
- [ ] Contact details published in privacy policy
- [ ] PDPO notification process understood for data breaches

---

## Step 3: Data inventory (record of processing)

Document every category of personal data you handle:

| Data category | Purpose | Legal basis | Retention period | Shared with |
|--------------|---------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| Customer names & phone numbers | Service delivery | Contract / consent | 5 years | Payment processor |
| Employee records | HR & payroll | Legal obligation | Duration + 7 years | Internal only |
| *(add rows)* | | | | |

- [ ] Data inventory completed for all departments
- [ ] Data flows mapped (collection → storage → sharing → deletion)
- [ ] Cross-border transfers identified and documented

---

## Step 4: Privacy policy essentials

Your public privacy policy should include:

- [ ] Who you are (legal name, contact, DPO details)
- [ ] What data you collect and why
- [ ] How long you retain data
- [ ] Who you share data with (processors, partners)
- [ ] Data subject rights (access, correction, deletion, objection)
- [ ] How to lodge a complaint with PDPO
- [ ] Cookie and tracking disclosure (if applicable)

---

## Step 5: Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)

Conduct a DPIA when processing is likely to result in high risk, such as:

- Large-scale processing of sensitive data (health, biometrics, financial)
- Systematic monitoring of public areas
- New technology with significant privacy implications

**DPIA template outline:**

1. Describe the processing activity
2. Assess necessity and proportionality
3. Identify risks to data subjects
4. Define mitigation measures
5. Document sign-off by DPO and management

- [ ] DPIA completed for highest-risk processing activities
- [ ] Mitigations implemented and tracked

---

## Step 6: Operational controls checklist

- [ ] Consent mechanisms are clear and opt-in where required
- [ ] Data subject request process defined (30-day response target)
- [ ] Breach notification procedure documented (72-hour PDPO notification where applicable)
- [ ] Staff trained on data handling and phishing awareness
- [ ] Vendor contracts include data protection clauses

---

## Step 7: Ongoing compliance

| Activity | Frequency |
|----------|-----------|
| Privacy policy review | Annual |
| Data inventory update | Quarterly or on system change |
| Staff refresher training | Annual |
| Vendor due diligence | On onboarding + annual review |
| Compliance self-audit | Annual |
