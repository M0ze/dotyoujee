# NISF 2026 Readiness Checklist for Ugandan SMEs

A practical self-assessment for small and medium organizations preparing to align with Uganda's National Information Security Framework (NISF) 2026 — especially those supplying or partnering with government entities.

> **Disclaimer:** This checklist is for educational purposes. It does not constitute legal advice or a formal audit. Contact NexUpTech for a full NISF gap analysis.

---

## 1. Governance & accountability

- [ ] A named person (or role) is responsible for information security
- [ ] Security responsibilities are documented and communicated to staff
- [ ] Leadership reviews security risks at least quarterly
- [ ] Security policies exist and staff have acknowledged them

## 2. Risk management

- [ ] Critical information assets are identified and inventoried
- [ ] A basic risk register exists with owners and treatment plans
- [ ] Third-party vendors with access to sensitive data are listed and reviewed
- [ ] Business impact of a security incident has been considered

## 3. Access control

- [ ] Unique user accounts (no shared logins) for all systems
- [ ] Strong password policy enforced (or MFA where available)
- [ ] Admin/privileged access is limited and reviewed periodically
- [ ] Offboarding removes access on the same day an employee leaves

## 4. Data protection

- [ ] Personal and sensitive data is classified (at least: public / internal / confidential)
- [ ] Encryption is used for data in transit (HTTPS, VPN, encrypted email where needed)
- [ ] Backups are tested and stored securely (offline or immutable where possible)
- [ ] Data retention and disposal procedures exist

## 5. Operations & monitoring

- [ ] Operating systems and applications are patched regularly
- [ ] Antivirus or endpoint protection is deployed on all workstations
- [ ] Logs are retained for critical systems (minimum 90 days recommended)
- [ ] Unauthorized software installation is restricted

## 6. Incident response

- [ ] An incident response contact list exists (internal + external support)
- [ ] Staff know how to report suspected phishing, malware, or data leaks
- [ ] A basic incident playbook covers: contain → assess → notify → recover
- [ ] PDPO notification process is understood if personal data is involved

## 7. Physical & environmental

- [ ] Server/network equipment is in a locked or access-controlled area
- [ ] Visitor access to sensitive areas is logged
- [ ] Laptops and mobile devices have screen lock and remote wipe capability

## 8. Awareness & training

- [ ] New staff receive security orientation within their first week
- [ ] Phishing and social engineering awareness training is conducted annually
- [ ] Policies are available in a language staff understand

|-------|----------------|

| 50–79% | Moderate gaps — prioritize access control, backups, and incident response |
